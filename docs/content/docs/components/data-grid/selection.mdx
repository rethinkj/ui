A `DataGrid.Selection` holds the grid's cursor, the anchor that Shift extends from, and the selected ranges. The app keeps it in its state; the grid changes it in its handlers, as a person selects. Pass it with `options().select(selection)` to make the grid selectable; most other options need it.

A million selected rows are one range, so selecting everything costs nothing.

## What the user does

- A press selects at the moment the button goes down. Shift extends from the anchor, Control adds or removes.
- Dragging extends to the cell under the pointer; beyond an edge it scrolls one step per movement of the pointer.
- A press on a row number selects the row.
- The arrows, Page Up and Page Down, Home and End move the cursor; with Shift they extend. Control+A selects all. The rows and columns follow the cursor.

## What the app reads and does

| Method | |
| --- | --- |
| `mode()`, `row()`, `column()` | The mode and the cursor's cell |
| `anchorRow()`, `anchorColumn()` | Where Shift extends from |
| `ranges()` | The selected `Range`s: `top`, `left`, `bottom`, `right`, inclusive; `Range.ALL` columns for whole rows |
| `contains(row, column)`, `isRowSelected(row)`, `isEmpty()`, `rowCount()` | Questions |
| `select(row, column)`, `extend(row, column)`, `toggle(row, column)`, `selectAll(rowCount)`, `clear()` | Select from the app's own handlers |
| `reset()` | Clear everything and scroll back to the start, for a new result |

With `options().changes(changes)`, the selection follows its rows by their keys when the app sorts, filters, inserts or removes rows, so Delete, Control+Minus, Control+0 and a paste act on the rows the user selected. A range that a sort tears apart becomes several; when the cursor's row is gone the cursor goes to the nearest row left, and when every selected row is gone the selection collapses to the cursor. A selection of more than `Selection.FOLLOWED_ROWS` (10 000) rows collapses to the cursor when its rows move. Without keys the selection stays on its row indexes, so an app that reorders rows calls `clear()` or `reset()`.

The selection also remembers where the grid is scrolled to, so a grid built again with it, after its tab was away or in another window, comes back to the same rows and columns. One selection belongs to one grid at a time.

`onSelect(handler)` tells the app when the selection changes, for a status line such as "3 cells selected". `DataGrid.cellCount(ranges, columns)` counts them.

## Copying

`options().copy(clipboard)` lets Control+C and Control+Insert put the selection on the clipboard as tab-separated lines: the rows and columns that have a selected cell, in quotes only where a tab, line break or quote needs them, and around an empty field alone on the last line, which would otherwise read as no line. Pass `app.clipboard()`, which is the system's clipboard in a window and an in-memory one under a script.

| Option | |
| --- | --- |
| `nullText(String)` | What a copied `NULL` is; `""` unless set, as spreadsheets read an empty cell. |
| `copyLimit(cells)` | The grid copies at most this many cells itself; 100 000 unless set. |
| `onLargeCopy(handler)` | Hands a larger copy, or one with rows not loaded, to the handler with its `Copy` (ranges, headers, cell count) and the effective cell limit. Setting the handler leaves the limit unchanged. Build the text with `DataGrid.copyText` on another thread, fetch it from the database, or tell the user why it was not copied. |
| `copyLimit(cells, handler)` | Sets the limit and a handler that receives the `Copy`, as before. |
| `contextMenu(state, items)` | Adds Copy and Copy with headers to the grid's menu, before your items. |

`DataGrid.copyText(columns, ranges, headers, nullText)` builds the same text from your code, and `DataGrid.parseText(text)` reads it back into rows of cells.
