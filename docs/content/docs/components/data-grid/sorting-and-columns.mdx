The grid shows sort keys and lets the user change them, resize columns, and choose, move, hide and freeze them. It sorts nothing and keeps nothing: the app gets the new keys, widths and layout, keeps them and gives them back on the next build. That way sorting can happen in the database, where a million rows live.

Javadoc: [`DataGrid.SortKey`](/api/com/rethinkj/ui/kit/DataGrid.SortKey.html), [`DataGrid.Layout`](/api/com/rethinkj/ui/kit/DataGrid.Layout.html), [`DataGrid.Fit`](/api/com/rethinkj/ui/kit/DataGrid.Fit.html).

## Sorting

`options().sort(keys, onSort)` shows a list of `SortKey(column, descending)` in the header: an arrow per key, numbered when there are several. A press on a sortable column's header cycles ascending, descending and unsorted, and replaces the keys. Shift with a press adds the column as a further key, or cycles it. `onSort` gets the new list. The arrows need `rethinkj-ui-icons`.

## Resizing

`options().onResize((column, width) -> …)` lets the user drag the right edge of a header cell, and fit a column to its content with a double press on the edge. A dragged width stays above the theme's `grid().minColumn()`; a fitted one also stays within `maxColumn()`. The app keeps the widths and passes them to the columns.

## Fitting columns

`options().fitColumns(fit)` makes every column as wide as its header and the cells of the loaded rows, once per result, as a double press on its edge does. So a table opens with its headers and values whole. The widths stay the app's: the grid hands each one to `onResize`, which the option needs (without it, building the view throws). `fit` is a `DataGrid.Fit` in the app's state beside the widths. `fit.reset()` fits every column again, for a new result, where the app also calls `Selection.reset()`.


- The fit happens on the first frame with loaded rows. The first frame shows the widths the app gave, the next the fitted ones, so a script needs one `frame` more before a `snap` shows them.
- With `loading`, it waits for the first page. A grid without rows waits for its rows.
- Each width stays within the theme's `grid().minColumn()` and `maxColumn()`, so a column of long text does not take the whole grid.
- A column the user resized before the fit is left as it is. A column that a `Layout` hides is fitted when it is first shown, unless the app gave it another width meanwhile.
- It measures every column, also those out of view sideways.

## Choosing, moving and hiding

A `DataGrid.Layout`, made with the titles of all columns and kept in the state, holds their order and which are hidden. With `options().columns(layout)`:

- the grid shows the layout of all the columns it gets;
- dragging a header cell moves its column, with a line where it will go;
- the header's context menu has Sort ascending, Sort descending, Clear sort (with `sort`), Filter… (with `filters`), Hide column, Freeze up to here (with `onFreeze`; Unfreeze columns on a frozen column), Fit width (with `onResize`) and Columns…;
- `DataGrid.columnsButton(layout)` is a toolbar button that opens the chooser: a checkbox per column, a search field beyond eight columns, Show all and Reset. Alt+Arrow Up and Down move the focused column.

With a layout, every handler, sort key and filter speaks of columns by their index among all of them; only the selection's ranges count the columns shown. `layout.shown(all)` gives the shown columns in order, and `hide`, `show`, `showAll`, `reset` and `moveBefore` change it from code.

## Freezing

`options().frozen(count)` keeps the first `count` columns, and the row numbers, at the left while the rest scroll sideways. `onFreeze(count -> …)` lets the header's menu change it.

## Reading the layout

Two kinds of column index meet in a grid with a layout. The columns the app passes to `dataGrid` are *all* columns, and handlers, sort keys, filters, `onResize`, `onFreeze` and the cells of `GridChanges` speak of them by their index in that list. The selection's `Range`s, `Selection.column()` and the keys in a tree dump (`column-<i>`, `cell-<row>-<i>`) count the columns *shown*, in the order shown. `shownColumns()` turns one into the other: `layout.shownColumns()[selection.column()]` is the index among all columns of the cursor's column.

`layout.toggleChooser()` opens or closes the chooser on the `columnsButton`, as a press on it does, for a menu item or a key of the app's. The other methods of a layout are on [`DataGrid.Layout`](/api/com/rethinkj/ui/kit/DataGrid.Layout.html).
