`Dialog.dialog` is a modal overlay in the middle of the window: a title, the content and buttons at the bottom right, over a scrim that dims the rest. It takes the focus as it opens, keeps it inside, and gives it back when it closes. Use it to ask before something that cannot be undone, or for a short form. For something light that need not block the window, use a [Popover](/docs/components/popover); for a message that goes by itself, a [Toast](/docs/components/toast).

Whether it is open is the app's state: the root attaches it with `root.overlay(open ? dialog : null)`.

| Parameter | |
| --- | --- |
| `title` | The heading, and the dialog's label |
| `content` | Any element |
| `buttons` | Shown at the bottom right, in order |
| `defaultButton` | One of `buttons` that Enter presses while it is enabled (the variant with it) |
| `onDismiss` | Escape and a press on the scrim: cancel |

Enter without modifiers presses the default button from a field of the dialog; a focused button, checkbox or select presses itself, and a multi-line field keeps Enter for its line breaks. `Dialog.enterPresses(button)` is that key handler alone, for a window that is a dialog itself.

The dialog's key is `dialog`. Two dialogs open at once, as a question over a dialog, need keys of their own: chain `.key("discard")` onto the second, which then lies over the first with a scrim of its own; without it the frame throws, as for any two siblings with one key.

A frameless window's own title bar stays usable under a dialog. Closing the window closes the dialog without its dismiss handler.

## Options

`Dialog.dialog(theme, title, content, buttons, onDismiss, Dialog.options()…)` adds what a dialog has beyond its title: a mark of what it edits before the title, a badge or a close button at the head's end, and a secondary action or a status at the start of the button row.

| Option | |
| --- | --- |
| `leading(El)` | Before the title, `space().m()` from it, centred on its line; keeps its size |
| `trailing(El...)` | At the head's end; they keep their size and the title gives way, ending in an ellipsis with the whole in a tooltip |
| `start(El...)` | At the start of the button row, the buttons at its end; with no buttons the row is there for them alone |
| `defaultButton(El)` | One of `buttons` that Enter presses, as the parameter of the other form |

A dialog is as wide as its content, title and rows ask for, up to the window less `space().xl()` on each side: size it by sizing the content, as `field.w(320)`. The head comes after the content and the buttons in the order of Tab, so the dialog opens in its content's first field and a close button in the head is the last stop.

## Higher than the window

The dialog keeps `space().xl()` from the window's edges. Where its content does not fit, the content scrolls between the head and the buttons, which stay in view, with the theme's scrollbar; sideways too, for content wider than the window. A dialog that fits shows no bar and looks as before. The scroll area has room for the focus rings at the content's edges, so a field focused at the edge shows its ring whole, and Tab scrolls a field into view with its ring. The wheel scrolls the content, and Page Up and Page Down scroll it a page while the focus is on something that does not take them, as a button.

## Keyboard

| Keys | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Tab, Shift+Tab | Go around inside the dialog |
| Enter | Press the default button |
| Escape | `onDismiss` |
| Page Up, Page Down | Scroll content higher than the window |
